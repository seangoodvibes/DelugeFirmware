# Synth, kit and audio clip freezing

While a synth, kit or audio clip is playing, hold its pad in Grid view, press SELECT to open Clip Settings, and enable **[ ] Freeze**. Disable **[x] Freeze** to restore live rendering. The clip keeps its original type, source material, effects and automation.

For an audio clip, freezing captures one complete processed loop, including pitch/time-stretch processing, reversed sample playback, clip effects and automation. Frozen playback bypasses the original sample voice, time stretcher and effect chain. The original sample, markers and settings remain available when thawed; the cache never replaces the source file. Loaded clips in Player and Sampler modes are supported. Looper/FX mode, empty clips and clips being recorded are excluded because live monitoring and overdubbing cannot be represented by a repeating cache. Enabling monitoring or starting recording thaws the clip. As with kits, track recording and stem export restore the live path to preserve their recording taps. Independently clocked modulation effects use the longer best-effort snapshot described below. Evolving delay/reverb or sidechain inputs remain snapshots rather than guarantees of future equivalence.

Starting at the next clip boundary, the complete deterministic note pattern is captured to temporary 24-bit WAV files on the SD card. The instrument continues playing while recording and while the files finish writing. Playback switches at a subsequent boundary with the same iteration phase, after the loop starts have been prepared for streaming. For example, first-of-two notes require two clip loops; combined two- and three-loop conditions require six. Independent row lengths, ping-pong rows and routed tempo-synced global LFOs also contribute to this period. Even, triplet and dotted sync are included; fractional tick periods are kept exact when calculating a common clip boundary. Capture is limited to 64 clip loops and the streaming file-size limit. This can take several loops. Frozen playback bypasses voices, arpeggiators and the instrument effect renderer. For kits, the capture includes all sound rows, their effects and the kit’s affect-entire effects. It uses the same `VoiceSample` streaming infrastructure as audio clips, without creating an AudioClip or AudioOutput.

The stereo output and mono reverb send are recorded separately. Playback mixes the send into the song's shared reverb bus; it does not bake the other tracks or the shared reverb output into the clip. A recorder attached to the synth output also receives frozen playback.

## Scope and behavior

- Supports forward synth, audio-only kit and audio clips running on either the internal clock or an external clock. Reverse/ping-pong clip sequencing and clips currently recording cannot be frozen. Reversing an audio clip's sample is supported independently of clip sequencing direction. Arrangement recording is excluded.
- Kits containing MIDI or gate rows cannot be frozen: their external events cannot be represented by audio. Starting kit output recording or stem export thaws the kit automatically, preserving the recording tap before kit pan/compression/volume and isolated drum exports.
- Ordinary note-triggered sidechain sends remain live while frozen. Sidechain-sending sounds using arpeggiators or legato cannot be frozen because those triggers depend on the bypassed voice/arp state.
- This is a session-local cache. Saving the song preserves its editable instrument data, not the freeze cache. Reloaded songs and cloned clips use live rendering.
- Stopping and restarting a completed freeze retains its cache unless it is a non-snapshot cache containing a routed global LFO. Global LFOs follow the song clock, so those caches thaw on stop, seek or relaunch to avoid replaying an incorrect relative phase; enable Freeze again after restarting. Interrupting the capture discards the incomplete pass. Changing clip length, output, mono/stereo rendering mode, or playback direction invalidates the cache and restores synthesis. Tempo changes retain the recording and use pitch-preserving time stretching.
- Freeze preserves deterministic note iteration patterns, including silent iterations. Random note probability, FIRST/LAST and FILL conditions use a best-effort snapshot of the actual performance during capture; later random choices or FILL changes are not reproduced. Note-row timing continues while frozen so thaw does not reset iteration counters. Edits and live notes are not heard through the frozen instrument; unfreeze to hear them, then freeze again to capture them.
- Routed global LFO1/LFO3 support sine, triangle, square and saw waves with tempo sync. Deterministic local LFO2/LFO4 restart per voice and support free rates as well as sync; local LFOs on legato sounds use a best-effort snapshot.
- Free-running global LFOs, routed sample-and-hold/random-walk/warbler LFOs, random patch sources, modulation effects with independent clocks, DX7 oscillators, and drone notes that keep their voices across clip boundaries automatically use **best-effort snapshots**. This applies to synths, kit sounds and audio-clip modulation effects. The original renderer runs normally throughout capture; oscillator and modulation settings are not simplified or replaced.
- A best-effort snapshot targets at least eight clip loops, rounded up to a multiple of the complete known note/LFO pattern (a six-loop pattern becomes twelve loops). The 64-loop and file-size bounds still apply; when necessary it uses the largest complete pattern count that fits. If even one complete known pattern cannot fit, freezing still reports an error. Unlike exact song-clock-dependent caches, snapshots may be reused after transport restart: their recorded phase is intentionally part of the performance.
- Every cache records a continuation beyond its musical end: up to 2,048 samples (about 46 ms at 44.1 kHz), plus one render buffer of guard audio. At a whole-pattern boundary, the outgoing streams play that continuation while fresh streams fade in from the original start. The extra audio is **not** part of the musical loop length or restart-position calculation. Automatic sample-file looping is disabled, avoiding an early hard wrap before the sequencer boundary.
- The overlap uses smooth weights and bounded, smoothed power compensation to reduce phase-cancellation dips in sustained notes. Dry stereo audio and the reverb send use the same weights and gain. There are normally two streaming voices, temporarily four during the overlap; the outgoing pair is released afterward. Compensation is capped at +12 dB and the result is saturated. Synth/kit live-to-cache handoff now uses the same compensated fade while the native voices remain active; they are stopped after the fade. Audio-clip handoff itself is unchanged.
- Drone notes use the longer snapshot even with deterministic LFOs: their oscillator phases and unison beating continue across clip loops. A musical clip boundary alone does not imply that a held voice's waveform repeats there.
- This is still not a guarantee of bit-identical arbitrary future audio: random oscillator phases, noise, free-running arpeggios, changing sidechain inputs and evolving delay/reverb state can vary. Only supported note and modulation periods are calculated; these remaining states are snapshots.
- Synth and kit effects and their automation are baked into the captured audio. Shared reverb remains live, but the recorded send includes the song-volume scaling present during capture. Unfreeze and capture again after changing that scaling if the send balance needs to change.
- Resource allocation, card errors and streaming failures return to synthesis. Temporary files and sample/cluster references are released when the freeze is discarded or the clip is destroyed. File deletion runs in the SD card routine.
- As with an audio bounce, nonperiodic modulation and evolving tails become a repeating recorded performance. Overlapping the actual waveforms reduces join artifacts, but it cannot reproduce unlimited future randomness or make every evolving timbre identical to the unfrozen instrument. Near-opposite phases can still exceed the bounded compensation; listen to sustained patches as well as checking note timing.

## Crossfade A/B and drone handoffs

`tests/integration/clip_freeze/compare_crossfades.py` compares identical native passages at one- and eight-second loop lengths. It compiles the **production** cache controller against the host SD/stream doubles, rather than reimplementing its fade in Python. The other variants are a modeled 100-sample linear pre-margin fade, 100- and 2,048-sample linear post-margin fades, and a 2,048-sample equal-power fade. Thirteen sources (seven controlled tones, four earlier drone captures, Blade Vang and Brick Speedboat) produce 130 cases. Inputs are the saved native PCM captures; no new random performance is substituted between variants. Results and common-level listening files are in `build/clip-freeze/crossfade-ab/`.

The local join measurements show a tradeoff, not a universal winner. The following eight-second cases use the maximum sample step around the known join divided by the largest native step in the corresponding source neighborhoods. The level column is the minimum 10 ms RMS relative to the weighted native source energy, separating cancellation from natural envelope movement:

| Source | Audio-style 100-sample fade: step / level | Production freeze fade: step / level |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 31.5625 Hz, opposite-phase join | 2.61 / 0.990 | 1.36 / 0.474 |
| 55.5625 Hz, opposite-phase join | 1.89 / 0.995 | 1.21 / 0.599 |
| Blade Vang | 0.86 / 0.943 | 0.82 / 0.952 |
| Brick Speedboat | 1.00 / 0.951 | 0.76 / 0.983 |

The short fade confines cancellation to a shorter interval but can introduce a sharper bass edge. The long compensated fade reduces that edge, but exact/near-opposite phases still exceed its bounded gain correction. Ordinary long linear fades had deeper dips; equal-power fades could boost phase-aligned tones by about 3 dB and did not solve opposite-phase cancellation. The compensated loop fade is retained; these results do **not** establish inaudibility for arbitrary patches. A phase-aligned splice would need separate testing for timing and transient displacement before adoption.

`run_crossfade_delugemu.py --source blade --seconds 8` (and `brick`, plus `tone-55.5 --seconds 1`) verifies the actual AudioClip path. It plays the same source with a pre-margin, then plays the host production-controller result with no pre-margin, avoiding a second native fade. GDB confirms `VoiceSample::fudgeTimeStretchingToAvoidClick` is reached at unity speed; observed fades began 93–97 samples before the boundary. Captures are taken after detaching and settling, and all runs check for firmware aborts. This checks real AudioClip behavior and playback of the production fade; it is distinct from the end-to-end synth freezing runs. The 55.5 Hz emulator control had maximum step/RMS 0.0281 for native looping versus 0.01136 for the freeze fade, while minimum 10 ms level/median was 0.952 versus 0.618. Whole-passage preset metrics include natural waveform edges and are not perceptual scores.

The supplied presets also exposed two separate handoff problems:

- Entering freeze previously stopped live voices immediately, jumping to another recorded oscillator/LFO phase. Synths and kits keep the native render active for a compensated handoff (250 ms for snapshots, 2,048 samples otherwise). Notes and arpeggios continue through that handoff; afterward the native voices are stopped and cached playback bypasses synthesis.
- Unfreezing used ordinary late-note catching, which can reject a held wave-oscillator note or be disabled by Catch Notes settings. Notes already logically held during freezing now restart immediately, while retaining mute/audition checks and the current iteration decisions.
- For a held drone, cache playback continues until a complete musical-pattern boundary, then the ordinary native renderer restarts inaudibly. Readiness compares stereo power and four broad frequency bands, rather than loudness alone. After at least 250 ms of native rendering and 100 ms of continuous matching, a 250 ms compensated fade returns to synthesis. If no match is found after 30 seconds of native rendering, a one-second fallback fade completes the request. The initial boundary wait can add up to one captured pattern. The menu shows `[~] Unfreezing` during this process. Non-drone thawing retains an immediate return to native rendering.
- The cache continues wrapping during both the boundary wait and native warmup. Re-enabling Freeze during either phase cancels the thaw, reuses the existing files and releases any newly rebuilt native voices. Stop and invalidation cancel the bridge; streaming failures fall back to native rendering. No native voices are parked while fully frozen and no engine-specific DSP checkpoints are retained. Native rendering and cache streaming overlap temporarily during unfreezing.
- Readiness is a heuristic, not an equivalence test. Random oscillator phases, unison beating, free-running modulation, noise and effect history can differ after restart. The timeout deliberately permits an imperfect handoff rather than leaving the request pending indefinitely.

`run_delugemu.py --drone --patch 'SDCard Backup/Blade Vang.XML'` and the equivalent Brick Speedboat command capture live, frozen, freeze-entry and thaw audio. `analyze_handoffs.py` produces common-gain thaw before/after files in `build/clip-freeze/handoff-comparison/`. These are different live performances, so the measurements describe dropouts rather than sample equivalence. The lowest 100 ms level relative to the first frozen second improved from 0.067 to 0.451 for Blade Vang and 0.081 to 0.603 for Brick Speedboat. Those earlier baseline captures spent 0.4 s and 0.2 s below 20% of that reference respectively; neither fixed capture did. Natural modulation still changes the level, and restarting synthesis does not restore its historical oscillator, LFO or effect state.

The earlier handoff implementation passed 19 host scenarios with address/undefined-behavior sanitizers, including cache-to-live warmup, dry/send mixing, wrapping during thaw, streaming failure and stopping during thaw. Both audio-comparison unit tests and final Debug/Release builds passed. Emulator runs passed for both supplied drone patches, the three native AudioClip A/Bs, the alternating-cycle synth/sample kit and the audio-clip regression. Final follow-up runs `handoff-brick-final` and `handoff-kit-final` passed after allowing note/arp events during the entry fade. `--trace-handoff` uses a temporary breakpoint after capture to locate the first 2,048-sample handoff and saves an approximately centered four-second `freeze-entry.wav`; the breakpoint is removed before capturing that transition. ### Delayed handoff validation

`transition_spectrum.py` compares the heard thaw against the corresponding phase of the preceding frozen cycle using Hann-windowed FFT octave-band powers. It reports gain separately from normalized spectral shape and exports common-gain `thaw-heard.wav` and `thaw-frozen-reference.wav`. This is independent of the firmware readiness filters. Timing comes from captured frame bounds and native-wait/warmup sample counts, with audio buffering uncertainty; the metric is diagnostic, not proof of perceptual identity. Unit tests check a known FFT tone, gain invariance, and detection of a different tone with equal power.

A preliminary delayed implementation could pass its four-band readiness check briefly while still differing in the independent FFT comparison. Brick Speedboat naturally also varies between unfrozen eight-second cycles: the initial diagnostic measured about 5.27 dB mean normalized octave-shape difference between live cycles (Blade Vang: 2.06 dB). These observations are why neither a level match nor a spectral score alone is described as seamlessness.

## Tempo changes and external clock

Frozen playback derives its playback rate from the measured recorded frame count, its complete musical period, and the current clock. It uses the existing `VoiceSample`/`TimeStretcher` path at unchanged pitch. Both dry stereo and mono reverb-send streams share the same musical clock and stretch decision. External-clock estimates may change without invalidating the cache; Freeze can also be enabled while following external clock.

Native-rate playback retains the recorded-continuation crossfade. Once time stretching or drift correction becomes necessary, the two sample streams use native time-stretcher loop handling and update their sync anchors at each complete pattern boundary. They do not allocate a second outgoing pair of time stretchers at every wrap. Returning to unity retains the paired stretchers until playback stops/restarts or the cache is cleared, so one stream cannot exit grain playback ahead of the other. An exhausted read head restarts through the same readiness check used by AudioClip. No synth voices or synth DSP state are retained while frozen.

The musical marker excludes the extra recorded continuation. A native-rate wrap also limits its crossfade to the continuation that is actually unread: slight clock drift can consume part of that margin before the boundary. Tempo changes during capture produce a recording of that performed pass; replay stretches the whole pass, as for a resampled audio clip, rather than retroactively correcting every note's timing.

The Brick Speedboat 120→130 BPM investigation found an additional failure in the initial implementation: an outgoing time-stretched read head exhausted the file during the old fixed-tail fade, causing a silent fallback to live synthesis. A debugger breakpoint identified the outgoing render failure. Continuous native time-stretcher loop handling replaced that combination; the comparison harness explicitly verifies that the result remains frozen with zero synth voices, rather than accepting an audible native fallback as a successful freeze test.

`run_tempo_ab.py` uses a one-bar drone at 120 BPM and an eight-loop (16-second) cache, then changes to 130 BPM. It records the same native pass both as a full-output resample and as the freeze cache, plays the resample through an ordinary AudioClip, records the frozen stream at 130, and separately renders the synth at 130. An additional AudioClip plays the exact dry cache WAV. Completed files are read from the guest FAT32 image without mounting or modifying it. No live synth is present in the AudioClip-only comparison songs. `analyze_tempo_ab.py` reports gain separately from aligned spectral and envelope differences and exports explicitly gain-matched listening files. The full-output resample bakes shared reverb/master output, whereas freeze preserves a live shared reverb send; the native 130 performance also has different random/unison and evolving-effect history. These comparisons are not expected to null exactly.

The completed comparison is in `build/clip-freeze/brick-tempo-ab-continuous/`. The source-pass alignment correlation was 0.991. The 130 BPM frozen capture remained in PLAYING state with an active time stretcher and zero native synth voices over multiple complete patterns. The captures use the emulator output tap rather than the front-panel resampling recorder; the AudioClip-only song provides the isolation of muting the original synth.
