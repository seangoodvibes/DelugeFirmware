# Synth, kit and audio clip freezing

While a synth, kit or audio clip is playing, hold its pad in Grid view, press SELECT to open Clip Settings, and enable **[ ] Freeze**. Disable **[x] Freeze** to restore live rendering. The clip keeps its original type, source material, effects and automation.

For an audio clip, freezing captures one complete processed loop, including pitch/time-stretch processing, reversed sample playback, clip effects and automation. Frozen playback bypasses the original sample voice, time stretcher and effect chain. The original sample, markers and settings remain available when thawed; the cache never replaces the source file. Loaded clips in Player and Sampler modes are supported. Looper/FX mode, empty clips and clips being recorded are excluded because live monitoring and overdubbing cannot be represented by a repeating cache. Enabling monitoring or starting recording thaws the clip. As with kits, track recording and stem export restore the live path to preserve their recording taps. Independently clocked modulation effects use the longer best-effort snapshot described below. Evolving delay/reverb or sidechain inputs remain snapshots rather than guarantees of future equivalence.

Starting at the next clip boundary, the complete deterministic note pattern is captured to temporary 24-bit WAV files on the SD card. The instrument continues playing while recording and while the files finish writing. Playback switches at a subsequent boundary with the same iteration phase, after the loop starts have been prepared for streaming. For example, first-of-two notes require two clip loops; combined two- and three-loop conditions require six. Independent row lengths, ping-pong rows and routed tempo-synced global LFOs also contribute to this period. Even, triplet and dotted sync are included; fractional tick periods are kept exact when calculating a common clip boundary. Capture is limited to 64 clip loops and the streaming file-size limit. This can take several loops. Frozen playback bypasses voices, arpeggiators and the instrument effect renderer. For kits, the capture includes all sound rows, their effects and the kit’s affect-entire effects. It uses the same `VoiceSample` streaming infrastructure as audio clips, without creating an AudioClip or AudioOutput.

The stereo output and mono reverb send are recorded separately. Playback mixes the send into the song's shared reverb bus; it does not bake the other tracks or the shared reverb output into the clip. A recorder attached to the synth output also receives frozen playback.

## Scope and behavior

- Supports forward synth, audio-only kit and audio clips running on the internal clock. External clock, reverse/ping-pong clip sequencing, and clips currently recording cannot be frozen. Reversing an audio clip's sample is supported independently of clip sequencing direction. Arrangement recording is excluded.
- Kits containing MIDI or gate rows cannot be frozen: their external events cannot be represented by audio. Starting kit output recording or stem export thaws the kit automatically, preserving the recording tap before kit pan/compression/volume and isolated drum exports.
- Ordinary note-triggered sidechain sends remain live while frozen. Sidechain-sending sounds using arpeggiators or legato cannot be frozen because those triggers depend on the bypassed voice/arp state.
- This is a session-local cache. Saving the song preserves its editable instrument data, not the freeze cache. Reloaded songs and cloned clips use live rendering.
- Stopping and restarting a completed freeze retains its cache unless it is a non-snapshot cache containing a routed global LFO. Global LFOs follow the song clock, so those caches thaw on stop, seek or relaunch to avoid replaying an incorrect relative phase; enable Freeze again after restarting. Interrupting the capture discards the incomplete pass. Changing tempo, clip length, output, mono/stereo rendering mode, or playback direction invalidates the cache and restores synthesis.
- Freeze preserves deterministic note iteration patterns, including silent iterations. Random note probability, FIRST/LAST and FILL conditions use a best-effort snapshot of the actual performance during capture; later random choices or FILL changes are not reproduced. Note-row timing continues while frozen so thaw does not reset iteration counters. Edits and live notes are not heard through the frozen instrument; unfreeze to hear them, then freeze again to capture them.
- Routed global LFO1/LFO3 support sine, triangle, square and saw waves with tempo sync. Deterministic local LFO2/LFO4 restart per voice and support free rates as well as sync; local LFOs on legato sounds use a best-effort snapshot.
- Free-running global LFOs, routed sample-and-hold/random-walk/warbler LFOs, random patch sources, modulation effects with independent clocks, DX7 oscillators, and drone notes that keep their voices across clip boundaries automatically use **best-effort snapshots**. This applies to synths, kit sounds and audio-clip modulation effects. The original renderer runs normally throughout capture; oscillator and modulation settings are not simplified or replaced.
- A best-effort snapshot targets at least eight clip loops, rounded up to a multiple of the complete known note/LFO pattern (a six-loop pattern becomes twelve loops). The 64-loop and file-size bounds still apply; when necessary it uses the largest complete pattern count that fits. If even one complete known pattern cannot fit, freezing still reports an error. Unlike exact song-clock-dependent caches, snapshots may be reused after transport restart: their recorded phase is intentionally part of the performance.
- Every cache records a continuation beyond its musical end: up to 2,048 samples (about 46 ms at 44.1 kHz), plus one render buffer of guard audio. At a whole-pattern boundary, the outgoing streams play that continuation while fresh streams fade in from the original start. The extra audio is **not** part of the musical loop length or restart-position calculation. Automatic sample-file looping is disabled, avoiding an early hard wrap before the sequencer boundary.
- The overlap uses smooth weights and bounded, smoothed power compensation to reduce phase-cancellation dips in sustained notes. Dry stereo audio and the reverb send use the same weights and gain. There are normally two streaming voices, temporarily four during the overlap; the outgoing pair is released afterward. Compensation is capped at +12 dB and the result is saturated. The initial live-to-cache handoff is not crossfaded.
- Drone notes use the longer snapshot even with deterministic LFOs: their oscillator phases and unison beating continue across clip loops. A musical clip boundary alone does not imply that a held voice's waveform repeats there.
- This is still not a guarantee of bit-identical arbitrary future audio: random oscillator phases, noise, free-running arpeggios, changing sidechain inputs and evolving delay/reverb state can vary. Only supported note and modulation periods are calculated; these remaining states are snapshots.
- Synth and kit effects and their automation are baked into the captured audio. Shared reverb remains live, but the recorded send includes the song-volume scaling present during capture. Unfreeze and capture again after changing that scaling if the send balance needs to change.
- Resource allocation, card errors and streaming failures return to synthesis. Temporary files and sample/cluster references are released when the freeze is discarded or the clip is destroyed. File deletion runs in the SD card routine.
- As with an audio bounce, nonperiodic modulation and evolving tails become a repeating recorded performance. Overlapping the actual waveforms reduces join artifacts, but it cannot reproduce unlimited future randomness or make every evolving timbre identical to the unfrozen instrument. Near-opposite phases can still exceed the bounded compensation; listen to sustained patches as well as checking note timing.

## Verification

Run `python3 tests/integration/clip_freeze/run.py` for host tests of the production cache controller with SD and audio boundary doubles. These cover delayed finalization, sample gain, stereo/send separation, packed-mono playback and output-mode invalidation, track recording, stop/restart offsets, duplicate sequencer events, invalidation, partial read failures, start failures, card errors, allocation failures and cleanup. The runner enables address and undefined-behavior sanitizers. These tests do not exercise real SD timing or the hardware mixer.

Run `python3 tests/integration/clip_freeze/run_delugemu.py --dry` for the emulator regression (requires the sibling `delugemu` checkout and `build/Debug/deluge.elf`). Omit `--dry` to include delay and reverb. The test uses the Python front-panel interface to enable and disable Freeze; GDB reads cache and recorder state from the emulated machine. It captures audio without using host speakers. Results, screenshots and audio go under `build/clip-freeze/emulator/`. Use `--select-direction 1` if your emulator's SELECT polarity matches the firmware; the local emulator used here requires `-1`.

Add `--kit --dry --iterations 2,3` to test a kit containing a synth drum and a generated WAV sample drum, including the six-loop iteration pattern. Use `--kit --compare-fx --iterations 2,3` to check gain and note timing with row delay and row/kit reverb. Wet waveforms can vary even between live cycles; that run reports the live-cycle variation alongside the before/after comparison, rather than applying the dry waveform threshold.

Add `--dry --iterations 2` to verify alternating audible/silent loops, or `--dry --iterations 2,3` for a six-loop pattern. The runner uses the emulator’s instruction-counted clock to keep CPU and audio timing together, leaves capture uninterrupted, and waits for rendered frames before measuring. It may run slower than real time. The test compares multi-cycle envelopes, note/silence activity, gain and aligned note waveforms, and checks cached playback phase before and after transport restart. Raw live/frozen PCM and measured errors are saved with the results. These are tolerance-based checks of a deterministic fixture, not a claim of bit-identical arbitrary synth output.

Use `--lfo ID:wave:syncLevel:syncType` (repeatable) to add controlled LFO routing, `--expected-loops N` for the independently expected full period, and `--continuous` for global-LFO transport invalidation. The fixture's file sync levels are converted by the firmware according to its tick magnitude. `--lfo-config` changes settings without adding routing. `--snapshot --expected-loops N` compares non-repeating sounds against the native passage around the capture boundary, rather than a different random performance. `--expect-rejected` remains useful for external MIDI/gate kits and live-monitoring audio clips.

`--patch 'SDCard Backup/SYNTHS/SYNTHS/158 Tempo-Synced LFO.XML' --continuous` loads the supplied preset into the test song. Legacy multi-root XML is converted for the fixture; original files are untouched. Sample dependencies are copied from `--sample-root` (default `SDCard Backup`). Unless explicitly requested, preset oscillator/effect settings are retained. The strict waveform test applies to controlled dry fixtures; real presets with random oscillator phase/unison are lifecycle tests unless a comparison is explicitly requested.

Add `--deterministic-oscillators --dry` to test a preset variant with reset oscillator phases, one unison voice, and delay/reverb disabled. This isolates modulation from random oscillator interference; it does not establish waveform equivalence for the unmodified preset. For a three-loop dotted LFO test of preset 158, also pass `--lfo-config 1:sine:1:19 --expected-loops 3`.

The emulator regression checks the actual handoff and renderer bypass, dry audio gain, stop/restart, thaw, recorder cleanup, and tempo invalidation. It does not estimate real-device CPU percentages. The host tests cover failure injection independently.

For best-effort validation, use `--dry --dx7 --lfo 1:sah:1:0 --lfo 3:warbler:1:0 --lfo 2:rwalk:0:0 --snapshot --expected-loops 8`. The test confirms the DX7 engine loaded and compares frozen audio to the native passage around the capture boundary. An earlier live performance is not a valid waveform reference for random modulation. Use `--kit --dry --mod-fx chorus --lfo 1:triangle:0:0 --iterations 2,3 --snapshot --expected-loops 12` for a free-running kit LFO plus chorus and a preserved six-loop note pattern; use `--audio --dry --mod-fx chorus --snapshot --expected-loops 8` for audio-track effects.

The comparator refines timing alignment to approximately 1 ms before checking 10 ms energy bins, and excludes waveform windows below four 24-bit quantization steps. Silence and note activity are still checked across the whole passage. Run `python3 tests/integration/clip_freeze/test_audio_equivalence.py` to verify that fractional-bin offsets pass while a missing note fails. Snapshot controller tests cover pattern rounding, file-size bounds, the recorded overlap, sustained-tone level continuity, outgoing-voice cleanup, and immediate restart at a different recorded phase.

The final snapshot emulator runs passed for DX7 plus sample-and-hold/warbler/random-walk LFOs (`snapshot-dx7-random-final`), a twelve-loop kit with free-running LFO and chorus (`snapshot-kit-final`), audio-track chorus with its processing buffer confirmed active (`snapshot-audio-chorus-active`), and the unchanged six-loop deterministic pattern (`snapshot-exact-regression`). Results and native/captured/frozen PCM are under `build/clip-freeze/`. Maximum aligned waveform relative RMS errors against the captured passage were 0.009%, 0.027%, and 0.103% for the three snapshot fixtures. Restart, thaw, tempo invalidation and recorder cleanup passed without firmware aborts. The controller suite passes 15 sanitizer scenarios, the comparator passes two positive/negative alignment tests, and Debug/Release builds pass. These results validate reproduction of the captured passage, not equality with future random live performances.

Use `--audio --dry` for a generated audio-clip fixture with two tones, silent gaps and unequal source channels. For sustained-note tests, add `--drone` to the emulator runner, optionally with `--patch` for a supplied preset. This replaces the note rows with one note at position zero whose length is the full clip, and expects an eight-loop snapshot unless `--expected-loops` specifies a different known pattern. It saves matched-gain `live.wav` and `frozen.wav`, raw PCM, and boundary-sensitive measurements. The maximum sample-step ratio is a useful click detector for the sine control; complex presets contain natural waveform edges, so it is not a universal perceptual score.

The drone investigation reproduced a repeatable sine discontinuity roughly 24 times its normal live sample step. An ordinary overlap removed that discontinuity but introduced a level dip to about 48% of the median short-window level. The compensated overlap kept that test around 96%, before extending drone capture to eight loops. The earlier short-note regression tests did not detect this problem because they measured attacks and overall gain rather than sustained loop joins.

Hardware checks before release:

1. Freeze a synth with notes crossing the loop boundary, stereo effects, delay, reverb and automated volume/pan. Compare capture and frozen playback levels, timing and channels; check the splice for clicks. Include a silent loop and long gaps between notes.
2. Run several other tracks and freeze multiple synth clips. Verify that no other track appears in a frozen clip, that the shared reverb continues, and that CPU load falls after the handoff.
3. Toggle Freeze repeatedly; stop during capture and during file finalization; restart a completed freeze both at the beginning and partway through. Unfreeze sustained notes and check that synthesis resumes.
4. Change tempo, clip length and playback direction. Switch between clips sharing an instrument, clone/delete frozen clips, undo deletion, and load another song. Check for stale voices, effect tails, memory/cluster errors and orphaned temporary files.
5. Record a frozen synth through the existing track recorder, and compare levels with recording the unfrozen synth.
6. Exercise SD full/removal, slow-card streaming, and low-memory conditions. Confirm that failure restores synthesis and that later freeze attempts work.
